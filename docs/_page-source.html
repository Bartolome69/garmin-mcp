Setting it up

  1. 01

    Open Claude’s connector settings

    On claude.ai: Settings → Connectors → Add custom connector.

    On the phone app: tap + beside the message box → Connectors → + (top right) → Add custom connector. No laptop needed at any point.

    Whichever you use, it reaches your other devices by itself.

  2. 02

    Paste this address

    Connector URL 
    https://garmin-mcp-1b4387.fly.dev/mcp

    Claude fills in the rest itself — it should show Sign in now selected. Leave it as it is and press Add.

  3. 03

    Sign in to Garmin

    Claude sends you to a sign-in page. Your Garmin email and password, plus a two-factor code if you use one. It returns you to Claude on its own.

    Rather not hand your Garmin login to a site that isn’t Garmin? Run the whole thing on your own machine instead.

    Your password is never stored. It goes straight to Garmin, is never written to disk, and is dropped from memory the moment Garmin accepts it. Only the access token Garmin issues is kept, encrypted.

  4. 04

    Ask it something

    Start a new chat. Real numbers back means you’re done — on the laptop and the phone, without setting up the phone.

    • Analyse"What did my last run look like? Break it down by kilometre."
    • Create"Build me a threshold session for Thursday and put it on my watch."
    • Analyse"How much of my last ride was actually in zone 2?"
    • Create"Put 3 × 10 minutes at threshold on the bike into Saturday."
    • Analyse"Am I running everything at the same effort? Check my last ten runs."
    • Create"Write me a push day — bench, overhead press, some triceps work."

What it can and can't touch

Your Garmin password is never stored. It goes to Garmin, and what comes back is an access token, kept encrypted on the server that runs this. Signing in again, or Disconnect, destroys it — either one takes the connection back straight away.

Reading is unrestricted: daily summaries, sleep, activities, splits, heart-rate zones. Writing stays inside the workout library — building a session, putting it on a date, taking it back off, and deleting one you're done with.

Deleting asks first, and the check isn't advisory: the first attempt only reads the workout's name back to you, and it takes a second one quoting that name to remove anything.

What it can't reach is your training history. No tool here deletes or edits a recorded activity, so a run you've actually done can't be lost through this, however badly a request is worded.

Make it a coach, not a lookup tool

Claude will answer questions about your data straight away. It gets sharper if you tell it how to work first.

Show the prompt

Nothing to fill in. Paste it in and it interviews you — reading your personal bests back from Garmin to check they’re current, asking how you feel against them, what you’re aiming at next, and what your real max heart rate is. Four questions, one message.

Once you’ve answered, paste your answers underneath it and later chats can skip the interview.

Training prompt 
You have access to my Garmin Connect account through the `garmin` MCP server. Be my running coach and training analyst.

Start with a short interview. Before you ask me anything, call get_profile and get_activities, so your questions come from evidence rather than blind. Then ask me, in a single message:

1. My personal bests. Read back what Garmin has and ask which are still right — it only knows races I ran wearing the watch, so some may be missing, and some may be years old.
2. How I feel against those bests right now: still in that shape, some way off it, or coming back from a break.
3. What distance I want to focus on next, and whether there's a race and a date.
4. My max heart rate, and whether that's from a real test or Garmin's age-based guess. Every heart-rate zone you'll see depends on that number, so if it's a guess, treat zone percentages with suspicion and lean on pace instead.

Group all of it into one message — don't spread it across five turns. Alongside the questions, tell me what you've already worked out from my recent training, and ask me what you've got wrong. Don't prescribe anything until I've answered.

Then, working with me:

- Always pull the data before forming a view. get_activities for recent sessions, get_activity_details for splits and heart-rate zones inside one run, get_daily_summary for resting heart rate and stress. Don't ask me for numbers you can look up, and don't reason from memory of earlier messages.
- Prescribe from what I can do now, not from my PBs.
- Be direct about what the data does and doesn't support. If three runs isn't enough to call a trend, say so rather than producing a confident narrative.
- Notice the boring things that matter: pace drifting up at the same heart rate, cadence dropping late in runs, every run landing in the same narrow effort band, resting heart rate creeping.
- If get_sleep_data comes back empty several nights running, I don't wear the watch overnight. Say so once, then stop building on recovery metrics I don't have.
- When I ask for a session, build it with create_workout and schedule it with schedule_workout so it reaches my watch. Show me the structure and wait for me to confirm — those write to my real account.
- If I want something gone, unschedule_workout takes it off the calendar and keeps it; delete_workout removes it for good and will make you confirm its name. Tell me what is about to go and wait for my answer before you confirm it.
- Prefer one specific, well-argued recommendation to a menu of options.
- You're not a doctor or physio. If something looks like an injury or health question, say so plainly and stop there.

Running it yourself instead

You don’t have to use the hosted version. The same tools run entirely on your own machine, with your Garmin session never leaving it — and on a work account where an administrator controls connectors, it’s the way in. It takes a Mac, a Terminal window and about ten minutes.

Terminal 
$ curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Bartolome69/garmin-mcp/main/scripts/bootstrap.sh | bash

It fetches the code, sets up Python, signs you in to Garmin and registers everything with Claude Desktop. Quit Claude fully first — Cmd + Q, not just the window. The repo has the detail, including Cursor, VS Code and Zed, which all speak the same protocol. This route is desktop only: a local server can’t reach your phone, which is the main reason the hosted version exists.

Questions

How technical is this, honestly?

Not very. You paste a link into Claude's settings, sign in to Garmin the way you would on any website, and that's it. No Terminal, no install, nothing to keep running on your computer.

My Claude account is through work — does that matter?

Yes. On Team and Enterprise plans only an administrator can add connectors, so a member can't do this themselves.

A personal account — free is fine — sidesteps it, and keeps your health data off a work login, which is where it belongs anyway.

It mentioned being rate limited, or a 429

The most common one, and it isn't your password. Garmin blocks sign-ins by network address before it ever checks your credentials. Office wifi, university networks and VPNs get hit hardest, because everyone on them shares a single address.

The fix that works: turn off wifi, tether to your phone's hotspot, run the command again. You only need the clean connection for that one sign-in — after that the saved session does the work.

Claude can't find any Garmin tools

An existing chat keeps talking to the connection it started with, so the first thing to try is simply a new chat. That fixes it more often than anything else.

If it still can't see them, open Settings → Connectors and check the Garmin entry is there and connected. Removing it and adding it again is safe — you'll just sign in to Garmin once more.

On a free Claude account you get one custom connector. If you already have another one, it holds the only slot and this won't add until you remove it.

On a work account — Team or Enterprise — members can't add connectors at all; an administrator has to. A personal account avoids that.

Running it yourself failed, mentioning Rust, OpenSSL or a compiler

Your macOS is too old. The floor is macOS 10.15 (Catalina), and it's a hard one — the Python this uses simply won't start below it. Updating macOS is the only fix. Any Mac from 2012 onwards can run Catalina.

Check it under the Apple menu → About This Mac. Updating macOS fixes it.

Garmin is asking for a two-factor code I never got

That's Garmin's two-factor. The code goes to the email on your Garmin account, or to your authenticator app — check spam if it's email. Run the command again once you have it; retrying costs nothing.

It says there's no sleep data

Your watch wasn't on your wrist overnight, or hasn't synced. Sleep, HRV and overnight body battery only exist if you wear it while asleep. Runs, splits and heart rate all work regardless.

My data looks out of date

Claude reads Garmin Connect, not the watch itself. Open the Garmin Connect app on your phone, let it sync, ask again.

How do I remove it?

Open the sign-in page and use Disconnect. That deletes the stored Garmin session outright, and the connection stops working straight away. Then remove the connector from Claude's settings.

Signing in again also retires whatever came before it, so if you ever think a connection has gone somewhere it shouldn't, signing in once more is enough to take it back.

Nothing changes on Garmin's side either way. If you ran it yourself instead, delete the garmin-mcp folder in your home directory and the hidden .garmin-mcp folder beside it.
